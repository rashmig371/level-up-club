QUALITY OVER PROBLEM COUNTS

Make every rep count.

Coding that holds up

Aim for 25–35 carefully reviewed problems plus 10–15 re-solves, not a race through a list. Use your strongest supported language (for example C# or Java); confirm language support with your recruiter.

Write complete syntax. Clarify inputs, state the invariant, test edge cases, and explain time and space complexity. Log misses and re-solve without peeking.

Design with reasons

Requirements → APIs and data → architecture → deep dive → failures, security, and rollout.

Compare alternatives and explain tradeoffs. State assumptions; estimate from them rather than inventing scale numbers. You do not need to spend the month memorizing AWS product names.

Stories, not scripts

Build 8–10 truthful examples using STAR: situation, task, action, result. Separate your contribution from the team's work.

Use measured results when available and honestly qualitative outcomes otherwise. Include real mistakes, disagreements, and learning—not canned leadership-principle phrases.

A useful mock rubric

Review clarity, correctness, testing, design tradeoffs, reliability, ownership, and evidence. Write one specific repair for each gap.

These are practice heuristics, not Amazon hiring cutoffs. Four weeks is a flexible plan, not a promise of L6 readiness. Confirm round format, tools, and expectations with your recruiter.
